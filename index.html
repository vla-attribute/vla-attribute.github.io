From detection to attribution

Because hardware offers no paired clean/corrupted rollout of the exact same state, there is no literal “video with the corruption removed” to show — that is precisely the intervention the counterfactual latent is designed to stand in for. Instead, the model reports, from the single observation it actually receives, a risk estimate and a ranked attribution over the present corruptions:

Presence
ρ̂k(ot)

which corruptions are in the current image

Risk
r̂(zc;zs)

probability the episode ends in failure

Attribution
Δ̂k

how much removing corruption k would lower that risk

On held-out failures with all six simulated corruptions present (K=6), ranking corruptions by Δ̂k correlates with the true solo-failure harm at r = 0.87, and the top-ranked corruption matches the true most-harmful one 85% of the time — far above a monolithic presence-and-failure model (which collapses to near-zero attribution because it can read corruption directly from the shared features) and a post-hoc VLM baseline. Full numbers are in the Results section below.
