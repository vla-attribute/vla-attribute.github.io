What Would Removing This Corruption Change?

Failure Attribution for Vision‑Language‑Action Models

When a VLA policy fails under multiple co-occurring visual corruptions, which one is actually responsible?

Detection isn't attribution

Sensitivity

OpenVLA drops from 76.5% to 1.1% success under viewpoint change, indicating the need for analysis of the effect of visual corruptions on VLAs.

Detection

Corruption presence doesn't imply responsibility. Corruptions can be relatively harmless or affect different policies differently.

Attribution (this work)

Remove one corruption, hold everything else fixed: how much does failure risk actually drop?

Success rate is computed directly from ground-truth task outcome, not model predictions. See the Attribution and Results sections below for the model's predicted risk and attribution numbers reported in the paper.

From detection to attribution

Because hardware offers no paired clean/corrupted rollout of the exact same state, there is no literal “video with the corruption removed” to show. That is precisely the scenario the latent intervention is designed to stand in for. Instead, the model reports, from the single observation it actually receives, a risk estimate and a ranked attribution over the present corruptions:

Presence
\(\hat\rho_k(\phi_t,\ell)\)

which corruptions are in the current image

Risk
\(\hat r(z_c; z_s)\)

probability the episode ends in failure

Attribution
\(\hat\Delta_k\)

how much removing corruption \(k\) would lower the risk

We evaluate attribution as a removal-effect estimate: how well \(\hat\Delta_k\) predicts the true drop in failure rate from removing each corruption. On OpenVLA our estimate reaches an attribution MAE of 0.095 (single) / 0.101 (multi) with 0.011 regret, and 0.019 / 0.064 / 0.005 on \(\pi_{0.5}\). These numbers are far below a monolithic presence-and-failure model (MAE 0.42, which collapses because it reads corruption straight from the shared features) and a post-hoc VLM baseline (MAE ≈0.21–0.25). Full numbers are in the Results section below.

Our pipeline

The model factorizes frozen SigLIP features into a scene latent \(z_s\) and a per-corruption latent \(z_c\) of K=6 blocks. Click an output below to trace which components produce it and which training losses make it trustworthy rather than just a detector.

Attribution \(\hat\Delta_k\) is the counterfactual “zero block k” contrast; the adversary, anchor, INLP and routing losses exist to make that contrast mean “remove corruption k” rather than “perturb a shared feature.”

Quantitative results

As reported in the paper.

Detection is the easy part

Corruption-presence accuracy is relatively trivial. Our model reaches 99.4% (OpenVLA) and 99.3% (\(\pi_{0.5}\)), and even the few-shot VLM clears 90%. Presence is easy, but it doesn't tell you what removing a corruption would change (the attribution results below).

Corruption attribution by method (OpenVLA)

Removal-effect estimation, on a 0–1 scale, mean over 5 seeds. Lower is better for all three: MAE of the attribution \(\hat\Delta_k\) against the true per-corruption removal effect (single- and multi-corruption conditions), and regret of the top-ranked repair on multi-corruption failures. Oracle (empirical lookup) = 0 by construction.

Failure recognition during execution (ROC-AUC)

Single-frame failure prediction on held-out OpenVLA episodes (Ours, SigLIP), at points through the rollout. The oracle assigns test-set empirical failure rates and is flat. On corrupted episodes our risk stays discriminative throughout. On nominal episodes it sharpens as execution reveals the outcome.

Hardware attribution (\(\pi_{0.5}\)–DROID, “put the cube in the bowl”)
